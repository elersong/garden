## does sm-2 account for session conditions?

Wozniak's original algorithm assumes sessions are uniform in cognitive load. That assumption breaks immediately under real conditions — studying at 11pm after a full day is categorically different from studying at 9am with fresh attention.

> Wozniak, P. A. (1990). *Optimization of learning.* SuperMemo Guru. https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Optimization_of_learning

## what does the algorithm actually optimize for?

Short-term recall performance, not long-term retention. The score you assign yourself after reviewing a card is a *performance signal under algorithm conditions* — not a signal about durable memory formation. The two feel similar in the moment, but they're measuring different phenomena.

This distinction matters because the entire scheduling logic is built on that self-assessment. If the assessment is measuring the wrong thing, the intervals are optimizing for the wrong outcome.

> Kornell, N., & Bjork, R. A. (2008). *Learning concepts and categories: Is spacing the enemy of induction?* Psychological Science, 19(6), 585–592. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02127.x
