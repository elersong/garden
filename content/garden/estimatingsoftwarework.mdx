## What does Fred Brooks Jr use as an estimation rule of thumb?

In _The Mythical Man-Month_, Brooks presents a very simple rule of thumb he claims has worked well for him over decades. While this text is nearly 50 years old, I wonder how much has really changed that might affect his given ratios.

- 1/6 Coding
- 2/6 Planning
- 1/4 Unit Testing
- 1/4 Integration Testing 

I can't lie... the idea of only spending `~17%` of time coding is a paradigm shift for me. Not insofar as that is sometimes an accurate representation of the coding/everything-else split, but planning it that way. It makes sense, though. With accurate enough planning, the questions and/or discrepancies that sidetrack progress can be avoided. The more time spent planning (to a point), the less time gets spent figuring-out while implementing. 

Lastly, there's a kind of beauty in the idea of an even split between making and verifying. Spending at least as much time testing for all use cases as I spent building an idea, the more durable the implementation will be. And the better-documented. 

> Brooks, F. P., Jr. (1975). The Mythical Man-Month: Essays on software engineering. Addison-Wesley. Chapter 2, The Tar Pit
