This note references chapters 1-3 of _The Mythical Man-Month_ by F. P. Brooks, Jr.

> "It takes 9 months to gestate a human child, but 9 women cannot complete it in 1 month." - Brooks

Brooks makes the argument that onboarding and intercommunication between stakeholders is the largest
contributor to why onboarding into a late project will do more harm than good. For every new person
added to a project, the number of communication channels scale as **f(n) = n(n-1)/2** (that's quadratic, btw).

## Ok, but if adding headcount works for other industries, what makes software special?

Because errors in software can be invisible until they compound across systems or departments. At that point
time has to be reallocated to diagnose and repair things that have already been previously addressed.

If two people misunderstood each other causing a software issue, then at most, one conversation has to take
place to find and reset expectations. If a problem arises within a team of 16, then up to f(16)=120 might 
have to take place to find the problem. Then, 120 conversations have to take place to disseminate the correct
understandings at all levels... this is the crux of why headcount slows teams.

## Is the goal, then, always the simplest solution?

No! The goal is eliminating any **accidental complexity** while accepting **essential complexity**!

Something I really need to find better solutions for is "unscheduled curiosity." This tends to add accidental
complexity to my personal projects (in my process, rather than my code), and results in them taking longer
than they ideally have to.

### One sentence summary: The main thing is to keep the main thing the main thing.
